Next-token models generate variable-length causal sequences, but accumulated prediction error can limit long rollouts, and their sampling procedure does not naturally support joint guidance over future tokens. Full-sequence diffusion supports such guidance while typically using a fixed-length, non-causal representation. Diffusion Forcing assigns independent noise levels to sequence tokens, allowing partially observed histories and partially generated futures to coexist within one denoising model \cite{chen2025diffusion}. This report distinguishes the paper's recurrent model from its released attention reimplementation, evaluates video stability and outlines the remaining planning experiments, and audits the likelihood argument. The completed video runs provide an initial stability result; planning evaluation remains pending. We also derive corrections to Appendix A.
