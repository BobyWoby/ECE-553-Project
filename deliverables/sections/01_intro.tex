Next-token models generate variable-length causal sequences, but accumulated prediction error can limit long rollouts, and their sampling procedure does not naturally support joint guidance over future tokens. Full-sequence diffusion supports such guidance while typically using a fixed-length, non-causal representation. Diffusion Forcing assigns independent noise levels to sequence tokens, allowing partially observed histories and partially generated futures to coexist within one denoising model \cite{chen2025diffusion}. This report distinguishes the paper's recurrent model from its released attention reimplementation, evaluates video stability and released-checkpoint planning, and audits the likelihood argument. Completed video and maze evaluations provide limited evidence under the released protocol. We also derive corrections to Appendix A.
